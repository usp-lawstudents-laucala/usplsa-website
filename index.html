Fijian Competition and Consumer Commission (FCCC)

The Fijian Competition and Consumer Commission (FCCC) is Fiji's independent regulator responsible for promoting fair competition, protecting consumer rights, and fostering efficient, transparent markets. FCCC enforces competition and consumer protection laws, investigates anti-competitive conduct, monitors market practices, regulates prices in designated sectors, and advocates for fair trading across the country. Through its regulatory and enforcement functions, FCCC helps ensure that consumers are treated fairly while encouraging businesses to compete on a level playing field, contributing to a stronger and more resilient Fijian economy. We encourage our students and members of the public to learn more about FCCC's work and to reach out to the Commission for information, guidance, or assistance on competition and consumer protection matters.

About USPLSA

USPLSA, the USP Law Students Association, is the official student body representing law students at the University of the South Pacific. We exist to unify law students, provide accessible academic resources, and foster a strong sense of community among future legal professionals.

Our Mission:

  • Advocate for student interests and welfare
  • Organize academic and professional development events
  • Facilitate peer support and mentorship programs
  • Build lasting networks among law students and alumni

Our Vision: To be the leading student association that nurtures exceptional legal professionals committed to justice and service in the Pacific region and beyond.

Inter-Tertiary Moot – Representing USP in Fiji

The Inter-Tertiary Moot is a mooting competition held within Fiji, bringing together law students from USP, Fiji National University (FNU), and The University of Fiji (UNIFIJI). These teams compete before a panel of judges comprising legal practitioners, academics, and occasionally members of the judiciary.

USP Performance Centre

Each team prepares written submissions and delivers oral arguments on a challenging legal problem.

Why participate?

  • Gain experience competing against students from other Fijian institutions
  • Network with legal professionals and fellow law students
  • Build confidence in oral argument and legal reasoning
  • Add a prestigious line to your CV

The winner of the USPLSA Internal Moot earns the right to represent USP at the Inter-Tertiary Moot in Fiji.

USPLSA Internal Moot – Your Pathway to Advocacy

The USPLSA Internal Moot is the flagship mooting competition within USP's School of Law, organized by the USP Law Students Association. Open to all enrolled law students, it serves as both a training ground for novice mooters and the official selection pathway for students wishing to represent USP at the Inter-Tertiary Moot in Fiji.

Participants are paired into teams and assigned a legal problem. Each team prepares written memorials and presents oral submissions before a panel of judges comprising senior law students, tutors, and legal practitioners.

What you will gain:

  • Hands-on experience in legal research and writing
  • Practice in structuring persuasive oral arguments
  • Feedback from experienced mooters and legal professionals
  • The opportunity to represent USP at the Inter-Tertiary Moot

The USPLSA Internal Moot is held in Semester 2. Registration opens at the start of the semester. No prior mooting experience is required.

IRAC and Referencing Workshop – Master the Foundations of Legal Writing

The IRAC (Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion) framework is the standard structure for legal problem-solving used in law schools, exams, and professional practice. Proper legal referencing is essential for avoiding plagiarism and maintaining academic integrity.

This workshop, organized by the USPLSA, will cover both referencing styles required in the USP School of Law:

  • AGLC (Australian Guide to Legal Citation) – for legal sources such as cases and legislation
  • SOL (School of Law) – for specific faculty requirements and non-legal sources

Topics include:

  • Identifying the legal Issue in a problem question
  • Finding and stating the relevant Rule of law
  • Applying the law to the facts (Application/Analysis)
  • Reaching a clear Conclusion
  • AGLC and SOL referencing: footnotes, cases, legislation, and secondary sources
  • Common mistakes and how to avoid them
